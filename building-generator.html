Common in Japanese architecture: ground-floor shop projects forward of the upper-floor footprint, OR upper floors overhang the ground floor (covered walkway / arcade). Adds an extra sub-assembly on the front face. Positive value = extends forward; negative = recessed under upper-floor overhang.

Flat cladding pieces sized to the underside of each eave overhang (one per eave side). Glue to the bottom of the overhang where it sticks out past the wall — covers the otherwise-bare core material visible from below. Sized to the overhang's slope distance for gabled roofs (so it covers the full sloped underside).
3 mm-wide trim strips that follow the roof's perimeter, hiding the exposed core material at the panel edges. Gabled: a chevron piece outlining each gable peak's V-shape (the "triangle on each gable") plus a straight strip along each eave (the "end cap"). Slanted: a parallelogram per slanted-top wall, with vertical short ends so the wall's top edge looks flat-finished rather than tapering inward to a thin point.
Cladding Style
Adds a lower skirt equal to the core/floor thickness so exterior cladding can cover the exposed edge of the floor panel. Openings that touch the floor continue through this skirt.
A second cladding panel per wall, glued to the inside face. Doors and windows are cut at the same wall positions so each opening lines up with the matching hole in the exterior cladding and the wall core. Independent style — pick a smooth interior finish even when the exterior is something textured.

Inter-Floor Core Panels
Horizontal core-stock panels at each floor height. Panels have tongues on 3 sides; the 4th side rests against the wall installed last.
The wall you'll attach last during assembly. This wall has no slots for inter-floor tongues — the panels' open edges rest against its inner face when you close up the building.
Floor Core Style
Optional wire-access floor mode. The outside floor outline and wall slots remain, but a centered rectangular opening is cut out so the floor becomes a donut/ring instead of a solid slab.
Useful for buildings without interiors: leaves a perimeter floor frame while making it easy to run lighting wires up through the building.
Distance from the outside floor edge to the rectangular wire opening. Outer tabs/slots are preserved.
When enabled, inter-floor panels use the same donut-style wire opening.
Rooftop Mechanical Room
A small penthouse on the roof for HVAC, elevator overrun, water tanks, or stairwell access. Tongue-and-slot connection to the main roof.
Negative X = west, positive X = east. Negative Y = front, positive Y = back. Auto-clamped to fit on roof.
Rooftop Shield Wall
A low parapet wall around the rooftop equipment area — common on Japanese commercial buildings to hide AC units and tanks from street level.
